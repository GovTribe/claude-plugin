# Proposal narratives

Draft proposal sections, RFQ narratives, capability statements and related plans using the buyer's questions and available company evidence.

## Workflow

1. **Identify the requested passage.** Reuse its requirement, evaluation factor, buyer headings and limits. Preserve required terminology and mandatory language. For a new draft, write the requested section; for a revision, start from the selected version and retain accepted content outside the requested change.
2. **Explain the work.** Lead with the answer, then describe execution and relevant proof. Use the details below that fit the request; allocate attention to the buyer's stated factors without inventing weights.

   | Narrative | Useful content |
   | --- | --- |
   | Executive summary | The buyer's need, proposed solution, specific benefits and strongest proof, consistent with the detailed response. |
   | Technical approach | Activities, sequence, responsible roles, inputs, outputs, interfaces and handling of relevant risks. Explain how the work gets done instead of paraphrasing the PWS. |
   | Management, staffing or retention | Responsibilities, coverage, qualifications, recruiting/retention measures, transition, escalation and continuity. Use supplied staffing and service boundaries; distinguish proposed measures from established company practices. |
   | Quality-control or delivery plan | Work checkpoints, acceptance criteria, responsible reviewer, defect correction and reporting. Tie them to the actual deliverables and supplied approach. |
   | Past performance | Actual performer and prime/subcontractor/individual role, customer, dates, scope, scale, relevant work and supported results. Explain the connection to the buyer's problem; an award alone establishes no satisfaction rating. |
   | Capability statement | Buyer-relevant services, substantiated experience, supported business/vehicle facts, capacity and supplied contact details. Fit the requested paragraph or page length; a capability statement serves a different purpose from a company research profile. |

3. **Make the proof relevant.** Connect experience to similar scope, users, operating conditions or technical challenges. Attribute partner and employee work accurately. Use supported outcomes rather than invented metrics or superlatives. When asked to recommend an approach, explain it prospectively; check [Source authority and claims](source-authority-and-claims.md) before implying new company capacity or commitments.
4. **Revise without changing the deal.** Preserve prices, quantities, dates, roles, coverage, exclusions and conditions. Shorten by removing repetition, not material qualifications; keep required source language when requested. Incorporate a supplied correction throughout affected passages without reopening settled answers. For review-only work, identify the passage, weakness and needed evidence instead of rewriting it.
5. **Check the requested answer.** Each assigned question belongs in its required section or volume; a cross-reference does not replace it. Reconcile shared facts across sections and count final words/characters using the requested convention. Keep missing evidence in the internal handoff. For a complete response or rendered page limit, use the relevant File Templates guide and native file checks.
