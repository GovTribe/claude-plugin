# Staffing and Labor Costs

## 1. Establish the labor question

Reuse the active scope, files, roles, locations, performance periods and company assumptions. Determine whether the question concerns staffing adequacy, wages, labor-category rates, wrap assumptions or delivery costs. Follow the steps needed for that question; complete supplied facts can support a bounded assessment. Reuse settled staffing when only costs or margin are changing.

Before arithmetic, classify each labor quantity as paid, productive or billable hours and each rate as wage, loaded cost, bill price, ceiling or derived ratio. Record the role, period, included costs and any unknown basis. Establish a stated conversion between hour bases before combining them.

For wrap questions, define the starting and ending amount bases and included costs. A numeric rate quotient can describe the supplied figures; interpret it as a cost or price wrap only when the corresponding labor and hour bases and allocation bridge are established. Derive a planning wrap from explicit assumptions; an actual company wrap requires its cost accounts and allocation bases.

Find missing requirements or wage determinations with [Search_Government_Files](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-government-files-mcp-tool); use [Search_User_Files](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-user-files-mcp-tool) for requested prior BOEs, staffing plans or company rates. Read controlling originals or relevant passages through [source-content retrieval](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/guides/vector-store-content-retrieval). Distinguish contractual staffing requirements, the proposed plan and historical estimates.

## 2. Estimate staffing when adequacy is asked or unresolved

Translate workload and service levels into hours by role, site and period. Account for concurrent coverage, shifts, management, transition overlap, surge and overtime where they affect delivery. For managed services, use volume and productivity; for fixed deliverables, use task effort and reviews.

Convert required productive work to FTEs using the stated productive capacity per FTE. For another workload basis, establish the corresponding capacity first. This establishes staffing capacity, not annual paid or billable hours. Treat leave, holidays, training, administration and vacancy consistently in that capacity without counting them twice; do not default to 2,080 productive hours. Explain how FTEs translate into people and shift coverage.

Compare the proposed staffing with this independent estimate, identify any shortfall or excess, and test the assumptions that could change the conclusion. A historical FTE count alone does not establish current staffing needs.

## 3. Research wages and comparable labor rates

Use [BLS_Occupational_Wage_Data](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/bls-occupational-wage-data-mcp-tool) for wage evidence. Preserve each requested role and map it to a defensible occupation or SOC proxy. Prefer supported local/MSA evidence, then state or national evidence as needed. Record geography, wage year, units, percentile and proxy limitations. Explain percentile choices; clearance, credential and recruiting premiums remain separate assumptions. Treat the benchmark as wage evidence, not a demonstrated company wage or wage floor. Establish any required or company floor from the governing labor requirement or supplied policy. BLS wages exclude modeled indirect costs and fee and cannot substitute for a required wage determination or CBA. Separate sourced labor requirements from competitive recruiting assumptions.

Use [Search_GSA_Labor_Rates](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-gsa-labor-rates-mcp-tool) when Schedule comparisons help. Match category, qualifications, worksite, clearance, contract and rate year; avoid averaging unrelated roles or treating sibling years as independent comparables. These are published ceilings, not wages or demonstrated task-order winning rates. Establish applicability and the same rate basis before interpreting a difference as ceiling headroom or compliance. For awarded labor evidence, use [Comparable Prices and Cost Drivers](comparable-prices-and-cost-drivers.md) and match the actual service unit and scope.

## 4. Check historical labor footprint where relevant

Use [Search_Service_Contract_Inventory](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-service-contract-inventory-mcp-tool) for reported hours, FTEs and workshare tied to the relevant award, vendor, buyer and fiscal year. Check documented coverage before comparing years or interpreting missing records.

Prime records carry source-row totals; subcontractor records support hours/FTE workshare, not subcontractor prices. Avoid adding subcontractor slices to totals that already include them. Compare the reported scope and period with the proposed work. SCI derived dollars per hour are blended footprint context, not a wage, labor-category rate or complete bid price.

## 5. Build labor costs and explain the assessment

Build compensation from wage or salary inputs on their established hour or annual basis; use a supported paid-to-productive bridge when deriving cost per productive hour. Apply a bill rate to billable hours for proposed revenue. Where these hours differ, show the bridge rather than reusing productive capacity for every calculation. Label conditional rate-times-hours products by their assumed basis instead of presenting them as established payroll or revenue.

Show direct compensation, fringe, overhead, G&A, escalation, travel, subcontractors and fee where applicable. Use company-approved allocation bases; avoid reapplying costs already included in a loaded rate. Distinguish markup or fee on cost from margin on sales. If missing bases prevent a labor-cost estimate, return the supported staffing findings and identify the missing inputs; an explicitly assumed scenario remains useful when its limits are clear.

Explain the supported staffing or rate finding, sources, assumptions and material sensitivities. Use [Pricing Model Workflow](pricing-model-workflow.md) for combined calculations or workbook revisions and [Price-to-Win Research and Modeling](price-to-win-analysis.md) for bid-price assessment. Carry the pricing evidence into `govtribe-capture-workflows` only when the task becomes a pursuit, teaming or competitive-position decision.
