# Federal Budget Data

## 1. Set the scope and years

Identify the agency, program, technology, or service. Reuse the requested timeframe; otherwise cover the next 24 months and the fiscal years containing those dates. Compare the latest budget publication with the previous available cycle.

Start with the [official tables and source links](./federal-budget-sources.md). Check the official hub for newer releases, amendments, or enacted funding before drawing current conclusions. Budget files are downloaded on demand; if the host cannot retrieve a needed file, ask for an attachment or report the resulting coverage gap.

## 2. Find the budget accounts and programs

Download the relevant OMB budget-authority and outlay workbooks from the source index. Search/filter them with the host’s native spreadsheet or code tools by agency, bureau, account, and program terms. Retain account identifiers and funding categories. Budget authority and outlays answer different questions; neither is an estimate of contract awards available to pursue.

For DoD, search P-1 procurement titles and R-1 research/development titles. Retain the appropriation account and Budget Line Item or Program Element identifier; line numbers can change between publications. Use the source index's O-1 operations/maintenance and C-1 construction links for those markets.

## 3. Read what the funding buys

Use the [agency appendix links](./federal-budget-appendix-index.md) for account descriptions and financing schedules. For program or project detail, open the agency's Congressional Budget Justification or relevant service/defense-wide book:

- **Procurement:** P-40/P-5 exhibits for equipment, quantities, costs, and delivery plans.
- **Research and development:** R-2/R-2A exhibits for projects, planned work, acquisition strategy, and milestones.
- **Operations and maintenance:** OP-5 narratives for services and explanations of program changes.
- **Military construction:** DD Form 1391 for the installation, project scope, cost, and schedule.

For civilian programs, read the account/program justification for planned acquisitions, grant competitions, implementation schedules, and explanations of funding changes.

## 4. Compare funding and plans

Compare consecutive fiscal years within the latest publication, then compare the same fiscal year across publication cycles. Match program identifiers and scope; explain reorganizations or renamed accounts before calculating changes. Show meaningful dollar, percentage, quantity, or schedule changes, including new and discontinued work. Read the justification to separate new funding, rescissions, and transfers between accounts.

Keep units and actual, enacted, requested, and projected amounts distinct. Preserve discretionary, mandatory, and supplemental components without adding them again to their reported totals. Use exact appropriations text for precise dollar comparisons; tables and summary schedules may be rounded. Confirm enacted amounts against the enacted law, and check amendments for subsequent changes. The source index explains the tables’ units and aggregation rules.

## 5. Connect funding to potential work

Search the program names, acronyms, and identifiers using [Search_Federal_Forecasts](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-federal-forecasts-mcp-tool) and [Search_Federal_Contract_Opportunities](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-federal-contract-opportunities-mcp-tool). For grants, use [Search_Federal_Grant_Programs](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-federal-grant-programs-mcp-tool) and [Search_Federal_Grant_Opportunities](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-federal-grant-opportunities-mcp-tool). Follow [Early Federal Procurement Signals](./find-early-federal-procurement-signals.md) for dates, attachments, and related records.

Check the original forecast’s value range and award window; normalized values may represent a range boundary rather than a point estimate.

Use [Search_Major_Defense_Programs](https://govtribe.com/docs/govtribe-for-agents/tools/search-major-defense-programs-mcp-tool) for acquisition context where relevant. Retrieve budget amounts from the source files.

## Report

Produce a GovCon business report explaining funding changes, what the government plans to buy or fund, timing, related opportunities, and useful next actions. Cite the official document and relevant table, page, or program identifier. Explain gaps that affect the conclusions.
